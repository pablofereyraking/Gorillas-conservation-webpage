Why Are Gorillas So Special?

Gorillas are among the most fascinating animals on Earth. They are known for their incredible strength, intelligence, and complex social relationships.

Despite their powerful appearance, gorillas can be gentle, affectionate, and protective of their families.

Their Incredible Intelligence

🧠 Problem Solving

Gorillas can learn from experience and solve problems in their environment.

❤️ Emotions

Gorillas show behaviors associated with affection, fear, and social bonds.

💬 Communication

They communicate through sounds, gestures, and facial expressions.

Discover the Four Types of Gorillas

There are two gorilla species and four recognized subspecies. Each lives in different regions of equatorial Africa.

Western Lowland Gorilla

Gorilla gorilla gorilla

The most widespread gorilla subspecies. They live in tropical forests and swampy areas of Central Africa.

Countries: Cameroon, Gabon, Republic of Congo, Central African Republic and Equatorial Guinea.

Protected areas: Nouabalé-Ndoki National Park and Dzanga-Sangha Protected Areas.

Cross River Gorilla

Gorilla gorilla diehli

One of the rarest gorilla subspecies. They inhabit mountainous forests and hills near the Nigeria-Cameroon border.

Countries: Nigeria and Cameroon.

Protected areas: Afi Mountain Wildlife Sanctuary, Cross River National Park and Kagwene Gorilla Sanctuary.

Eastern Lowland Gorilla

Gorilla beringei graueri

Also known as Grauer's gorilla. They inhabit tropical and mountainous forests in eastern Africa.

Country: Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Protected areas: Kahuzi-Biega National Park and Maiko National Park.

Mountain Gorilla

Gorilla beringei beringei

They live in high-altitude forests and are known for their thick fur, which helps them survive colder climates.

Countries: Uganda, Rwanda and Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Protected areas: Bwindi Impenetrable National Park, Mgahinga Gorilla National Park and Virunga National Park.

A Complex Social Life

Gorillas usually live in groups led by a dominant silverback. They develop strong relationships through communication, cooperation, and family bonds.

Young gorillas learn important social skills through playing and interacting with other members of their group.
